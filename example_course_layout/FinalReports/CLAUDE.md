This directory holds internship Final Report submissions (PDF or DOCX). Grade them
semi-automatically using the local Ollama model (see `grading_common.py` /
`grade_submissions.py` at the repo root).

## Grading checklist

This is an original, paraphrased summary of a Khalifa University internship-report
rubric, restated in checklist form for this tutorial — not a quotation of any
handbook text. Treat each line as one thing the model should check for independently;
a submission can pass some and fail others.

- **Length** — roughly 1,500 words. Short submissions should be flagged, not silently
  passed, even if every section is technically present.
- **Cover page** — a title page identifying the student, the host organization, and
  the internship period.
- **Abstract** — a short standalone summary near the front, before the table of
  contents.
- **Table of contents** — present, and positioned after the abstract.
- **Introduction** — present, positioned after the table of contents. A reader should
  be able to jump straight from the introduction to the conclusion and already have
  a rough sense of whether the internship went well.
- **Organization overview** — the first major section after the introduction
  describes the host organization and, where relevant, how its departments relate to
  one another (a diagram is a plus here, not a requirement).
- **Diagrams used appropriately** — figures, charts, or diagrams appear where they
  genuinely clarify something, not just in the organization section.
- **Clear sectioning** — the body is broken into labeled sections and subsections
  rather than one long undifferentiated narrative.
- **Page numbers** — present throughout.
- **Appendices** — supporting material (e.g. organizational materials, extended
  data) is attached as an appendix rather than stuffed into the main body.
- **Summary section** — a distinct summary exists somewhere in the document (this is
  separate from the abstract).
- **Conclusion that actually concludes** — the conclusion states, in terms a reader
  can evaluate, whether the internship was a success, what was learned, and what
  skills were gained or sharpened — not just a restatement of the work performed.

## Grading stance

Be evidence-based: only mark a criterion as satisfied when the extracted text
actually shows it, and say what you saw (or didn't see) for each one. A rubric
criterion that's merely "technically present" but extremely thin (e.g. a one-line
"conclusion" that doesn't say anything about outcomes) should be marked present but
noted as weak in the comments, rather than silently scored as a full pass.
